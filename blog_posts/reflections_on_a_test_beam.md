# Reflections on a Test Beam
**2026-08-13**

I spent a week at a test beam at CERN, here are my reflections on the experience.


First of all, if you are not working in particle physics, you might wonder what a test beam is.


Imagine a one-week hackathon, but instead of getting some credits for AI usage and being forced to use some startup's shitty SaaS prototype, you get a particle beam from the [Proton Synchrotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_Synchrotron).
If that doesn't sound cool enough, you also get an experimental area enclosed by thick concrete blocks to protect everyone outside from the radiation from the beam, as well as a control room with measurement equipment connected to both the Proton Synchrotron beam data and experimental area equipment. 
Inside such a control room, I spent most of the past week, solving hard problems together with incredible engineers and physicists.


With the larger goal of testing the new inner [tracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Muon_Solenoid#Tracker) for the high-luminosity upgrade of [CMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_Muon_Solenoid), we also tested a lot of other subsystems, and got some real physics data. Throughout the week, every 20 seconds two beeps sounded in the control room, signalling a spill: the beam sending particles through our test setup. I still hear the beeps in my head.


Some reflections:
- No matter how prepared you are before, your stuff will break. And if it doesn't break, somebody else's stuff will break, breaking your stuff.
- Debugging and fixing things under extreme time pressure is **hard**, but also very fun.
- Human communication is inefficient and prone to errors. This might be one of the largest bottlenecks during a test beam.
- Logbooks are good. Hacking at 2am feeling like a boss will not feel good the morning after unless you documented what you did.
- Keeping a good mood, even during bad times, might be the most important skill.


I'll finish this post with some poetry.


---
Just as computers run to the beat of the clock, humans at test beams run to the periodic double-beeps of beam spills.
